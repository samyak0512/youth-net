Our Board Members

The governing council behind YouthNet

YouthNet's board brings together entrepreneurs, lawyers, musicians and development professionals — people who left conventional careers to build something for Nagaland's youth.

BI

Bendang Imchen

Chairman

Proprietor of Elysium Enterprise, a Nagaland-based IT firm delivering custom software to government departments across the state. He completed his B.Tech and M.Tech in Computer Science at IIT Delhi, and co-founded Colosseum Tech Solutions, whose GPS-based fleet management platform serves clients including Microsoft, Deloitte and Tech Mahindra.

NC

Nuneseno Chase

Director & Board Member

A leader with over 15 years across youth empowerment, women's leadership and talent management, spanning national and international organisations, government bodies and academia. She holds an MBA in Rural Development & Entrepreneurship and has completed executive programmes at the London School of Economics and INSEAD.

TM

Theja Meru

Board Member

A musician and entrepreneur who played in the Chennai-based band Blood and Fire in the 1990s before founding the Rattle & Hum Music Society to connect musicians nationally and internationally. He built Kohima's well-known Dream Cafe and Heritage guest house, and chairs Nagaland's Task Force for Music and Arts.

JS

Joshua Sheqi

Board Member

An advocate at the District Court, Kohima and the Guwahati High Court. His advocacy work helped secure job reservations for persons with disabilities under the Nagaland Public Service Commission, following a 2019 High Court ruling on reserved posts.

LP

Lezo Putsure

Board Member & Former Director

Former Director of YouthNet, holding an MBA from London South Bank University and a background in strategy from Harvard Business School. An Acumen Fellowship-India alumnus, he now leads EduCentre Pvt. Ltd. as Founder & Director.

ZO

Zaren Odyuo

Board Member

Founder of Tribal Enterprise Pvt. Ltd. at age 23, now one of the state's fastest-growing retail businesses under the 'Gravity' and 'Hub' names, with exclusive Adidas and Benetton showrooms across three towns and around 50 local youth employed.
